AI writes the code and checks it. Fine. But who owns it?

Writing and checking are no longer the problem: AI handles both. What matters now is who owns the code once the PR is in production.

Follow-up to “AI writes the code. Fine. But who checks it?”.

The PR is in production. Who owns it?

In the previous article, we answered “who checks?”. The answer: AI. Several AIs, from different vendors, governed by a strict process.

AI writes. AI checks.

Then the pull request (PR) is merged. The code is deployed. It runs. At 3 a.m., something breaks. Who owns it?

Not the AI. AI is never an owner. Ownership is carried by a human, or nobody carries it.

Waterfall or agile, the same phases

The software lifecycle has changed tempo. Waterfall runs the phases once, from the need through to production. Agile plays them again, in short loops. What changes is the rhythm, and the size of the loops. The phases stay the same.

Waterfall or agile, we chain the same phases.

  • why: the reason for doing the work, the problem that justifies it.
  • what: what the software must do, the result we expect.
  • how: the way to build it.
  • build: write the code.
  • test: prove it does what was asked.
  • check: review it, look for what will break, secure it before production.
  • deploy: put it in production.
  • retest: once it is in production, check that it holds.

Waterfall lines these phases up. Agile loops them. A short loop contains all eight, just as a long cycle does. Neither method drops one, and neither adds one. Only the cadence changes.

The dividing line, in 2026

Two phases stay human: the why, and the what. Why this work exists. What, exactly, the software must do.

From the how on, AI can do the work. The how, the build, the test, the check, the deploy, the retest. Checking included: that is what “Who checks?” is about, and the answer has not changed.

The line sits high. It does not split the person with the idea from the person who codes. It splits the why and the what, which stay human, from everything after, which AI can take.

Ownership moves up to the what

Direct consequence. Execution, from the how through the retest, can be done by an AI. And an AI is never an owner. So ownership can no longer sit inside the execution. It moves up one step, to the what.

Whoever defines the what owns the pull request, and the deployed code. They said what the software must do. They answer for what runs in production.

A convenient sentence is going around, handy for getting yourself off the hook: “The AI did it.” No. You cannot be serious, or professional, without a human keeping ownership. AI does the work. It does not own it.

AI is never an owner.

First, name the owner

Carrying ownership starts with a definition. Who is the owner? Without that answer, nobody carries anything. Ownership stays empty, and “the AI did it” moves into the vacant seat.

Who validates the what? That person is the owner. Of the pull request, and of the code once it is deployed. Validating the what and carrying ownership are the same act. To carry ownership, you first have to say who carries it.

The trap: a what with no hands on production

The trap. Marketing defines the feature. It validates the what, so it should carry the ownership. But it cannot change anything in production, and it cannot fix anything there. And behind it, nobody takes ownership.

The what has an author. Production has nobody.

Then marketing has to delegate part of its ownership. To a developer, or to an ops or support team. Ownership is split by role.

  • Marketing owns the what.
  • Support owns production.

The 3 a.m. alert goes to the support team. Not to marketing, which is asleep, and which couldn’t step in anyway. That intervention is in the support team’s professional contract. It is their job.

At 3 a.m., support stabilizes production. The line between an incident and a bad spec is settled during the day, when the marketing owner wakes up.

A discussion between intelligent people.

Conclusion

“Who checks?” has its answer: AI, inside a process a human holds. “Who owns it?” has its own: the human who validates the what. And when that human cannot touch production, part of the ownership is delegated to the people whose job is to answer there, including at 3 a.m.

AI writes and checks. Ownership has a name, and that name is a human’s.
